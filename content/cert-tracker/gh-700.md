## About the GH-700 Exam

GH-700 is for people improving everyday business work with GitHub and GitHub Copilot. Think of a program manager organizing an intake queue, a business analyst turning requirements into a repeatable process, or a product manager checking whether an automated report meets its purpose.

The [official credential page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/agentic-ai-business-professional/) names the certification **GitHub Certified: Agentic AI Business Professional (beta)**. Its [GH-700 study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/gh-700) uses the exam title **Enhancing Operational Efficiency with Agentic AI**. These refer to the same GH-700 exam.

> **Before buying:** This is independent, original single-answer knowledge practice based on the October 2026 outline. It is not official GitHub exam content or a reproduction of the real assessment. GH-700 is in beta, and the official page says the exam may include interactive components. A practice-bank score does not predict an exam result. GitHub subscriptions and exam fees are not included.

## Who Should Take This Exam?

Business analysts, product managers, program managers, technical project managers and other business professionals who use generative AI and make decisions about quality, speed, risk and control.

Deep software-development experience is not the focus. Some GitHub familiarity helps, but candidates should also practise turning a business requirement into a clear task, checking an output and deciding when a person must intervene.

## Practice Exam: 250 Original Questions

Each question presents a hypothetical business task and asks for a decision. Coaching explains the mechanism, why the alternatives do not fit, a transferable study tip and a practical application. Links point to public documentation.

## Study Tips

1. **Name the surface.** Before answering, establish whether the task concerns the App, CLI, web, an automation or an Actions workflow.
2. **Trace the control.** Separate task instructions, tool permissions, approval decisions and how long an agent continues working.
3. **Define success first.** Give an automated task a checkable output and a clear point for human review.
4. **Practise safely.** Use a disposable repository and hypothetical data. Confirm what an action changes and what stopping or reverting actually does.
5. **Recheck current documentation.** Beta outlines and preview features can change; follow the linked source rather than treating a menu or command as universal.
