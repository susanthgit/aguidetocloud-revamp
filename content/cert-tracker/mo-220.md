## Turn an Answer Into Excel Practice

After answering, read the explanation and the reasons the other choices fail. Follow the Microsoft reference when a behavior is unfamiliar. Complete a practical task in Excel, change an input, and check that you can explain the result without looking at the answer.

The following exercises use hypothetical values in a new practice workbook. Do not use a file containing personal, payroll or confidential accounting information.

### Data: Preserve a Numeric Value While Changing Its Display

Enter `12450.5` in A1 and `=A1*2` in B1. Apply a monetary number format with two decimal places to A1.

**Check:** B1 still returns **24901**. The number format changes the display, not the stored number. Compare this with replacing A1 with a text description and explain why presentation and data type are different decisions.

### Trial Balance: Calculate a Difference

Create separate Debit and Credit columns. Enter debit amounts **1200** and **800**, and a credit amount **2000**. Calculate the sum of each column and then debit total minus credit total.

**Check:** the difference is **0**. Change the credit amount to **1900** and confirm the difference becomes **100**. A zero difference checks equality of the two totals; it does not prove that every account classification or transaction is correct.

### Statements: Distinguish Formatting From Rounding

Enter revenue **230000** and operating income **28759**. Divide income by revenue in a third cell, then display that result as a percentage with one decimal place. In a separate cell, use `ROUND` to round the ratio to four decimal places.

**Check:** the unrounded ratio is approximately **0.12503913**. Its formatted display is **12.5%**, while the rounded numeric result is **0.125**. Explain why changing a cell's format does not change the value used by a later calculation.

### Routine Activities: Check Straight-Line Depreciation

Use **27600** for asset cost, **3600** for salvage value and **6** annual periods for useful life. Calculate one year's depreciation with `=SLN(27600,3600,6)`.

**Check:** the result is **4000** per year. Six annual charges total **24000**, leaving the **3600** salvage value. Change useful life to **72** monthly periods and explain why the result is then a monthly charge, not an annual one.

## A Practical Study Routine

1. **Find a gap:** use the free preview to identify a concept you cannot explain confidently.
2. **Read the source:** check the linked Microsoft guidance and its platform requirements.
3. **Do the task:** practise in Excel and compare the result with an explicit expected value.
4. **Change an assumption:** alter a date, amount, reference or filter and predict what should change.
5. **Repeat without prompts:** complete a small accounting workflow from a plain-language requirement.
