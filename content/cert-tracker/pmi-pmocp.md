## About the PMI-PMOCP Exam

> Practise building, mandating and running a PMO that proves its value: organizational project management maturity, PMO strategy and governance, service design, delivery and enhancement.

Independent original PMI-PMOCP concept and application practice aligned to PMI's published six-domain Examination Content Outline, read directly from PMI's own PDF in September 2026. Four-option questions include explanations, wrong-answer reasoning and public-source references.

## Who Should Take This Exam?

The PMI-PMOCP is designed for **PMO leaders and managers, portfolio and programme office staff, and consultants who stand up or reshape PMOs**. It validates applied judgement across the full PMO lifecycle PMI's outline covers — mandate, strategy, design, service delivery, performance and improvement — rather than general project management practice.

**Prerequisites:** PMI requires both education and experience, and the PMO-specific education is not optional. You must complete 10 hours of PMO-specific education before you may apply. You must also hold a secondary degree plus three years of project experience gained within the past eight years, or an active PMP in good standing. PMI allows up to three attempts within a one-year eligibility period. Check PMI's current eligibility rules before applying; this practice bank does not provide the required education and does not count toward it.

**Typical study time:** 8-12 weeks of intensive study

## Exam Domains & Weights

The PMI-PMOCP exam covers **6 domains**. Focus your study time based on the weights below — higher-weighted domains have more exam questions.

| Domain | Weight | Practice Qs |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Organizational Development and Alignment | 16% | 40 |
| PMO Strategic Elements | 18% | 45 |
| PMO Design and Structuring | 18% | 45 |
| PMO Operation and Performance | 15% | 38 |
| PMO Enhancement and Effectiveness | 18% | 45 |
| People | 15% | 37 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **250** |

> 💡 **Study tip:** **PMO Strategic Elements**, **PMO Design and Structuring** and **PMO Enhancement and Effectiveness** tie as the heaviest domains (18% each) — together they are more than half the exam, so ground yourself there first: linking the PMO's strategy to organizational objectives, choosing structures and service portfolios that fit its mandate, and the measurement, maturity and continuous-improvement work that proves a PMO earns its place. **Organizational Development and Alignment** (16%) rewards scenario practice in organizational project management maturity, governance and stakeholder alignment. **PMO Operation and Performance** and **People** are the smallest domains (15% each), and neither is safe to skip — one tests the day-to-day running and performance of PMO services, the other the roles, capability and interpersonal work that make those services stick.
