## About the PMI-RMP Exam

> Practise risk strategy, identification, qualitative and quantitative analysis, response and monitoring.

Independent original PMI-RMP concept and application practice aligned to PMI's current five-domain Examination Content Outline, inspected September 2026. Four-option questions include explanations, wrong-answer reasoning and public-source references.

## Who Should Take This Exam?

The PMI-RMP is designed for **experienced professionals seeking advanced validation**. 2+ years of hands-on experience recommended.

**Prerequisites:** PMI offers three eligibility routes and does not mandate an accredited training provider: contact hours may come from PMI Authorized Training Partners, employers, training companies, distance learning or university courses, though self-directed study does not count. The routes are a secondary diploma with 36 months of project risk management experience in the last 5 years and 40 contact hours of risk education; a bachelor's degree or global equivalent with 24 months of experience and 30 contact hours; or a degree from a GAC-accredited programme with 12 months of experience and 30 contact hours. Check PMI's current eligibility rules; this practice bank does not provide qualifying contact hours.

**Typical study time:** 8-12 weeks of intensive study

## Exam Domains & Weights

The PMI-RMP exam covers **5 domains**. Focus your study time based on the weights below — higher-weighted domains have more exam questions.

| Domain | Weight | Practice Qs |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Risk Strategy and Planning | 22% | 55 |
| Risk Identification | 23% | 58 |
| Risk Analysis | 23% | 58 |
| Risk Response | 13% | 32 |
| Monitor and Close Risks | 19% | 47 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **250** |

> 💡 **Study tip:** **Risk Identification** and **Risk Analysis** tie as the heaviest domains (23% each) — together they are nearly half the exam, so ground yourself there first: structured identification techniques, risk register construction, qualitative probability-and-impact assessment, and the quantitative work of expected monetary value, simulation and sensitivity analysis. **Risk Strategy and Planning** (22%) sits close behind and rewards scenario practice in risk appetite, thresholds and the risk management plan. **Monitor and Close Risks** (19%) tests reassessment, audits and closure discipline. **Risk Response** (13%) is the smallest domain but is heavily tested on matching strategies to both threats and opportunities, and on owner accountability.
