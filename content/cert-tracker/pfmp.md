## About the PfMP exam

PfMP is about decisions taken across a whole portfolio: which components to fund, which to stop, and whether the current mix still serves the organization's strategy. A single program can be running well while the portfolio around it is misaligned, over-committed, or carrying more risk than the organization has appetite for.

This independent practice bank follows PMI's **PfMP Examination Content Outline**, read directly from PMI's published PDF and cross-checked against the official PfMP handbook in September 2026. It contains 250 original single-answer questions. These are not official PMI questions, recalled exam questions, or copies of vendor samples.

The outline records that its role-delineation study participants were not bound by *The Standard for Portfolio Management*, and that some tasks and knowledge areas are unique to the outline itself. This bank is scoped to the outline's own tasks and vocabulary, so it deliberately does not test edition-specific process names, input/output tables, or process numbering from that Standard.

## Who is PfMP for?

PfMP is an experienced portfolio-management credential, and PMI assesses it in **two separate evaluations**. Evaluation 1 is a panel review of the written experience summaries in your application. Evaluation 2 is the multiple-choice examination. Only Evaluation 2 is an exam.

Education plus portfolio and business-management experience requirements vary with the applicant's qualifications. Check [PMI's current PfMP eligibility and application requirements](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/portfolio-management-pfmp) before applying or paying for preparation.

**This bank practises Evaluation 2 only.** It provides no exam voucher, qualifying education, or certification, and it neither prepares nor assesses the application panel review.

## Domains and practice coverage

| Domain | Official exam weight | Practice questions |
|--------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Strategic Alignment | 25% | 63 |
| Governance | 20% | 50 |
| Portfolio Performance | 25% | 63 |
| Portfolio Risk Management | 15% | 37 |
| Communications Management | 15% | 37 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **250** |

The 63/50/63/37/37 allocation rounds the official weights to 250 questions, giving each tied rounding remainder to the earlier domain. It does not change PMI's weights, and it does not imply that the real exam contains 250 questions.

**Strategic alignment** covers keeping components — programs, projects, and operations — tied to organizational objectives and priorities, and recommending portfolio scenarios and an initial high-level roadmap. Funding a valuable component and funding the right component are different decisions.

**Governance** covers establishing the governance model, developing the portfolio management plan, authorizing components, and improving the processes decisions run through. An approval route supplied in a scenario is a fact about that organization, not a claim that every portfolio is governed the same way.

**Portfolio performance** covers managing the portfolio through the governance model's own processes, monitoring the consolidated components, balancing the mix, and reporting progress toward strategic objectives. The subject is the aggregate across components, not one program's schedule or one component's earned value.

**Portfolio risk management** covers balancing and managing portfolio risk against the organization's risk appetite so that decisions can be made. Aggregate exposure can breach appetite while each individual component's risk still looks acceptable on its own.

**Communications management** covers continuous communication with stakeholders, understanding needs and expectations, addressing issues as they occur, managing conflicting interests, and fostering engagement in portfolio decisions. An appropriate response depends on the actual audience, the evidence, and the portfolio's constraints.

The outline also lists 75 knowledge-and-skills items as a flat list with no published domain mapping. This bank spreads them across the domains as an authoring aid, so that grouping is ours and not PMI's.
