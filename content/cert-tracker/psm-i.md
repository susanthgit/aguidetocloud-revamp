### Understanding and Applying the Scrum Framework

- **Empiricism:** make work visible, inspect it and adapt based on what is learned.
- **Values:** use commitment, focus, openness, respect and courage to guide behavior.
- **Team:** distinguish the Scrum Master, Product Owner and Developers accountabilities; understand cross-functionality and self-management.
- **Events:** recognize each event's purpose and the Sprint's role as the container.
- **Artifacts:** connect the Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog and Increment to their commitments.
- **Done:** use the Definition of Done to determine whether work is part of a usable Increment.

### Developing People and Teams

- **Self-management:** the team decides how to organize its work within the framework.
- **Facilitation:** help people inspect and make progress without taking their decisions away.
- **Coaching:** support learning, team effectiveness and the organization's understanding of Scrum.
- **Professional accountability:** Developers adapt their plan and hold one another accountable.

### Managing Products with Agility

- **Product value:** the Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value.
- **Backlog management:** keep the Product Goal explicit, items understandable and the backlog ordered and transparent.
- **Stakeholders:** use the Sprint Review to inspect outcomes and collaborate on what to do next.
- **Forecasting:** discuss likely outcomes from observed progress and uncertainty; a forecast is not a guarantee.
- **Incremental delivery:** a Done Increment can be released when it makes sense; the Sprint Review is not a release approval gate.

## Framework reminders

### The accountabilities are distinct

The Product Owner is accountable for product value and effective Product Backlog management. The Developers create the Sprint plan, build quality into the work, adapt their plan and hold one another accountable. The Scrum Master helps establish Scrum and improve the team's and organization's effectiveness. A person may delegate work, but delegation does not automatically transfer accountability.

### Events have different purposes

Sprint Planning establishes the Sprint Goal, the work selected and an initial plan. The Daily Scrum is for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. The Sprint Review is a working session for the Scrum Team and stakeholders to inspect outcomes and adapt future work. The Sprint Retrospective focuses on improving quality and effectiveness. They are not interchangeable status meetings.

### Commitments make artifacts useful

The Product Goal is the long-term objective for the product and commitment associated with the Product Backlog. The Sprint Goal gives the Sprint Backlog a coherent objective. The Definition of Done describes the quality state required for an Increment. If an item does not meet the Definition of Done, it is not part of the Increment and returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration.

### Treat forecasts as forecasts

Past progress can inform a forecast, but it does not make future scope, dates or outcomes certain. Use newly learned information to adjust expectations. Do not present a forecast as a promise or use an optional chart or estimation technique as if Scrum requires it.
