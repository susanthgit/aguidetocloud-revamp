## About the EDRP v3 Exam

> Plan for disruption, recover technology safely, and keep the business operating when normal systems, sites, suppliers, or communications fail

The EC-Council Disaster Recovery Professional (EDRP v3) exam validates the ability to plan, execute, and maintain business continuity and disaster recovery. It starts with risk assessment, business impact analysis, recovery objectives, and continuity strategy. It then goes deep on backup and recovery: RAID and storage architectures, backup types and consistency, cloud and offsite copies, immutability, replication, recovery sites, failover and failback, cyber clean rooms, virtualization, system-state and bare-metal recovery, dependency sequencing, and survivable recovery metadata. The final domains cover disaster declaration, roles, communications, runbooks, exercises, corrective action, plan maintenance, training, and safety. Original practice questions. ## Who Should Take This Exam?

EDRP is designed for **business continuity and disaster recovery professionals, infrastructure and cloud administrators, backup and storage engineers, security-resilience leads, risk managers, recovery coordinators, and technology leaders responsible for service continuity**. It is especially useful if your role covers BIA, RTO/RPO design, backup policy, alternate-site planning, cyber recovery, DR exercises, or recovery governance.

**Typical study time:** 6-10 weeks of focused study

> 💡 **Study tip:** Keep the boundaries clear. **Risk assessment** asks what can happen; **BIA** asks what the business loses and how quickly it must recover. **RTO** is the restoration-time target; **RPO** is the acceptable data-loss interval. **Replication is not backup**, **RAID is not backup**, and a **snapshot is not automatically an independent backup**. For recovery strategy, know the difference between Backup and Restore, Pilot Light, Warm Standby, hot active/passive, and multi-site active/active. A recovered VM is not a recovered service until data, identity, networking, certificates, integrations, security, and capacity are validated.

## Study Tips

1. **Start with BIA and recovery objectives** — every backup and recovery design should trace back to process priority, RTO, RPO, and tolerable outage
2. **Learn the backup boundaries** — full vs incremental vs differential, snapshot vs backup, replication vs recovery point, immutable vs offline
3. **Practice dependency sequencing** — identity, networking, DNS, keys, data stores, middleware, applications, integrations, and traffic cutover
4. **Treat failback as its own operation** — reverse synchronization, conflict handling, validation, controlled cutover, and rollback all need a plan
5. **Know the exercise ladder** — desk review, tabletop, simulation, technical recovery, parallel test, and full interruption have different evidence and risk
6. **Use our practice exam** — try the 20 free questions first, then review every explanation and wrong-answer rationale
