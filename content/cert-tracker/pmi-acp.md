## About the PMI-ACP Exam

> Practise the agile mindset, servant leadership, product value and delivery flow.

Independent original PMI-ACP concept and application practice aligned to PMI's current four-domain Examination Content Outline, inspected September 2026. Four-option questions include explanations, wrong-answer reasoning and public-source references.

## Who Should Take This Exam?

The PMI-ACP is designed for **scrum masters, agile coaches, product owners, delivery leads and project managers** already working on agile teams. It validates applied judgement across the range of approaches PMI's outline covers — Scrum, Kanban, XP, Lean and hybrid delivery — rather than fluency in any single framework.

**Prerequisites:** PMI's current outline requires a secondary qualification, 21 hours of formal agile training and agile team experience. The experience route is 2 years of general project experience within the past 5 years plus agile team experience, with alternative routes for a GAC-accredited degree, an existing third-party agile certification or an active PMP. Check PMI's current eligibility rules; this practice bank does not provide qualifying training hours.

**Typical study time:** 8-12 weeks of intensive study

## Exam Domains & Weights

The PMI-ACP exam covers **4 domains**. Focus your study time based on the weights below — higher-weighted domains have more exam questions.

| Domain | Weight | Practice Qs |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Mindset | 28% | 70 |
| Leadership | 25% | 63 |
| Product | 19% | 47 |
| Delivery | 28% | 70 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **250** |

> 💡 **Study tip:** **Mindset** and **Delivery** tie as the heaviest domains (28% each) — together they are more than half the exam, so ground yourself there first: agile values and principles, servant leadership behaviours, and the flow, estimation and iteration mechanics teams actually run. **Leadership** (25%) rewards scenario practice in coaching, conflict handling and stakeholder engagement. **Product** (19%) is the smallest domain but the most concept-dense — value-driven delivery, backlog refinement, prioritisation and incremental validation are all heavily tested.
