## The question

Can MS Teams as part of Microsoft Business Basic enable chatting with external users?

## The answer

Hi — thanks for asking. Good news: this one already exists, so there's nothing to build. 🙂

**Short answer: yes** — as long as your Business Basic plan is one of the versions that includes Teams (more on that in a second). Chatting with people outside your organisation is a standard part of Teams, not an enterprise-only extra, and it's **on by default**.

**In most cases you don't need to change a single setting.** Open Teams, start a new chat, and type the person's **full email address** — if they're in another Microsoft 365 organisation, it usually just connects. The feature is called **external access** (or federation), and the default setting is **Allow all external domains**.

**The one thing to confirm first:** Business Basic is now sold in a **"with Teams"** and a **"without Teams"** version. If you're on the without-Teams one, you'd need Teams alongside it — **Teams Essentials** is about $4/user/month (USD, annual). Worth checking in the Microsoft 365 admin centre before anything else, because that's the only situation where the answer is "no". ([Compare Teams plans](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-teams/compare-microsoft-teams-options) · [my Business Basic notes](https://www.aguidetocloud.com/licensing/microsoft-365-business-basic/))

---

**What external chat can and can't do**

You get 1:1 and group chat, calls, meetings, video and presence with people in other organisations. Screen sharing works too, though it's most reliable in a 1:1 chat between two Teams work accounts.

⚠️ What it **can't** do: **share files**, or give the other person access to your teams, channels or SharePoint sites. It's conversation only — that catches people out, so it's worth knowing up front.

Admin controls live at **Teams admin center → Users → External access**.
📄 [Manage external meetings and chat](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/trusted-organizations-external-meetings-chat)

**If you do need to swap files**, there are two other routes — both included with Business Basic's Teams:

- **Guest access** — invite someone *into* a team so they can see files and channel conversations. Guests don't use one of your Microsoft 365 licences, though heavy guest usage can attract a small Microsoft Entra External ID charge above the free tier. Set up in **Users → Guest access**, plus matching sharing settings in Entra ID, Microsoft 365 Groups and SharePoint — it's the fiddliest of the three, because the switches live in several places. 📄 [Guest access in Teams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/guest-access)
- **Shared channels (Teams Connect)** — one channel both organisations work in, using their own accounts, with no guest account created. Needs an Entra admin in *both* organisations to set up cross-tenant trust, so it depends on the other side's cooperation.

---

**If chat isn't working, the order I'd check:**

1. **Both sides have to say yes.** Federation is two-way. If the other organisation blocks yours, or hasn't enabled external access for that person, chat quietly fails no matter what you set on your end.
2. **Careful with the allow list.** If you add even one domain to the **allowed** list, **every other domain is blocked** — people add a partner's domain and accidentally cut off everyone else. To block just a few, use the **blocked** list instead.
3. **Personal Microsoft accounts are a separate toggle** on the same page (on by default in commercial tenants, but not available in GCC, GCC High or DoD).
4. **Trial-only tenants are blocked by default** — if either side is on a trial, someone has to change it.

📄 The comparison table I'd bookmark: [External access vs guest access, side by side](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/communicate-with-users-from-other-organizations)

If you tell me what you're actually trying to do — just chat with a client, or properly collaborate on files with them — I'll point you at the right one. 🙂

— Sush
