Often called a “Cowork ROI” calculator — here’s the honest version. Instead of a hype multiple, we show the one number that’s hard to argue with: the break-even minutes a single Cowork task must save to cover the Copilot Credits it burns. If your task plausibly saves more than that, the credits pay for themselves in freed time.

{{/* ── Measurement protocol: the honest differentiator ── */}}

Get an honest number in five minutes

The result is only as good as your two inputs. Skip the guesswork and measure a real task — it’s quick:

  1. Pick one repeatable task you’d actually hand to Cowork.
  2. Run it 3–5 times in fresh sessions, through to an output you’d accept. After each, type /cost and note the credits. Enter the median above.
  3. Time the human version honestly — then subtract the minutes Cowork still costs you (prompting, waiting, reviewing, fixing). The difference is your minutes saved.
  4. Read the break-even, then check it at 25% usable share before you trust it.

That single measured task tells you more than any preset ever will.

Assumptions & sources

The maths. Break-even minutes = credit cost × 60 ÷ (loaded rate × usable share), where credit cost = credits × price. Capacity value = minutes × usable share × rate ÷ 60. “Value per $1” is a benefit-cost ratio, not ROI, and never the headline. Task volume deliberately isn’t an input — it cancels out of the ratio (for whole-tenant budgeting, use the Cowork Cost Calculator).

Credit price. $0.01 per Copilot Credit is Microsoft’s public pay-as-you-go rate; prepaid capacity is effectively cheaper. Credits and the Cost Management dashboard are documented in Usage-Based Billing & Copilot Credits (Microsoft Learn) and the Copilot Cowork GA announcement. The /cost figure is an approximate per-session total — see it in action; treat the admin Cost Management dashboard as the source of truth.

Benchmark minutes are priors, not Cowork data. The optional per-task-type minutes are external study estimates compiled in Microsoft’s open-source Cowork ROI Model (Analytics Hub). They come from heterogeneous GenAI, Copilot, and automation studies — not measurements of Cowork tasks — so they can be optimistic and none is preselected. Your measured figure always wins.

Cowork Credit Value Calculator (Cowork ROI Calculator)

Work out whether a Microsoft Copilot Cowork task is worth the Copilot Credits it burns. Enter the credits the task used — type /cost in Cowork to get them — and the tool shows the break-even minutes: how much time the task must save to cover its credit cost. Add the minutes it actually saved (net of your prompting and review time) to see if it clears break-even, the modelled capacity value, and the value per dollar of credits. Honest by design: break-even minutes rather than a hype ROI multiple, a usable-share of saved time defaulting to 50 percent with 25 and 100 percent sensitivity, capacity value not cash savings, and optional external study benchmarks that are priors, not Cowork measurements. Priced at $0.01 per credit pay-as-you-go, editable for prepaid. Pairs with the Cowork Cost Calculator and the Copilot seat ROI Calculator.
